TwyLapse manual

How to plan a night, set the exposure, and shoot a time-lapse from dusk to dawn.

Names in bold are what you see on the screen. This manual is for app version x.y.

1. Before you start

What you need

  • An Android phone running Android 8.0 or later.
  • A sturdy tripod. A time-lapse shows every bump.
  • Power for the whole night: a power bank for the phone, and spare batteries or a mains adapter for the camera.
  • To use your own camera: a Canon camera that supports CCAPI, and the Shoot with an external camera add-on (see chapter 11).
  • To shoot without leaving your phone by the camera: an M5Stack CoreS3 or M5StickS3, and the Control the camera from an external device add-on.

2. First launch and permissions

On first launch the app asks whether it may send crash records and usage statistics. Your photographs, the dates you shot, your coordinates and anything you type are never sent. Saying no changes nothing in the app, and you can change it later under Menu → Permissions and settings.

3. How TwyLapse plans a night

Three ideas are enough to use the app.

Shoot plan

One shoot: when, where, which way the camera points, which camera and lens, and the interval. From these the app works out the sun and the moon and builds a schedule.

Capture control

The schedule is a chain of time slots. Each slot has a capture control — the rule for its exposure.

Capture controlExposureWhen
DaytimeAuto (metered)While it is light
SunsetAuto, usually darker (negative EV) to keep the colour of the sunWhen the setting sun is in the frame
Into the nightAuto, gliding to the night exposure ⅓ stop at a timeEvening twilight (inserted automatically)
NightFixed — the same all nightWhen the sun is far enough below the horizon
Out of the nightAuto, starting from the night exposure and lowering it as the sky brightensMorning twilight (inserted automatically)
SunriseAuto, usually darkerWhen the rising sun is in the frame

Sunset and Sunrise are used only if the sun actually enters the frame, given the direction and lens. Otherwise that stretch of twilight is Daytime.

Defaults and templates

  • Capture control defaults (Menu → Defaults): your usual settings for Night, Sunset, Sunrise and Daytime. New plans start from them; changing a plan does not change the defaults.
  • Plan templates: a saved plan to start from, such as “Starscape (with sunrise)”. Make your own with Save as template in a plan’s ⋮ menu.

4. Making a shoot plan

Open Menu → Shoot plan and tap + New shoot plan, or start from Plan templates with Create a plan from this template.

The plan screen has three pages; swipe left and right between them: the overview, the Schedule, and the Simulation.

Start and End

Tap the date or the time to change it. A whole night usually runs from before sunset to after sunrise.

Location

The sun and moon times depend on where you are. Set the location in any of these ways:

  • + Use my location — the phone’s current position.
  • 📍 Pick on the map — tap the place on the map.
  • Paste coordinates — paste “latitude, longitude”. Coordinates copied from Google Maps work as they are.
  • Import a saved location — from Menu → Locations.

The altitude can be typed in, or looked up from the coordinates with 🗻 Look up from the coordinates (needs the internet).

Camera and lens

Pick from My gear. For the phone camera the lenses are the phone’s own cameras. For a zoom lens, set the focal length you will actually use — it affects the field of view and the NPF value.

Interval

The time from one shot to the next. Below it the app shows the NPF shutter speed (the longest exposure before stars start to trail) and the minimum interval. NPF is only a guide; it does not change the exposure.

  • The interval cannot be shorter than the longest shutter speed in the plan plus a margin. If you try, the app says so — shorten the shutter speed first.
  • “Can be set” is not the same as “runs smoothly”. Some cameras need a second or more extra to refresh live view. The capture report tells you whether the interval was enough.

Direction and elevation

Drag the arrow round the compass to set the direction the camera faces, and along the arc to set the elevation. Tap the number to type a precise value. Turn on Shoot in landscape if the camera is horizontal.

The app uses this to decide whether the sun enters the frame (and so whether Sunset or Sunrise is used).

Schedule

The left band shows the sun altitude and the twilight (civil, nautical, astronomical). The right band shows the capture controls in order, with the times and sun altitudes of each boundary, and moonrise and moonset.

Drag a boundary up or down to move it. Tap a capture control to edit it for this plan (chapter 5).

Simulation

Shows what is in the frame at a given time and direction — the sun, the moon and the Milky Way — so you can check the composition before you go. confirm what is drawn

Output (phone camera)

Under Output: Make a video automatically, Write DNG and Write JPEG. DNG frames are large (often 20–30 MB each); turn DNG off if space is short.

5. Adjusting the exposure

Each capture control can be changed for one plan (tap it in the schedule) or for all new plans (Menu → Defaults). Undo changes puts back what was there when you opened the screen; Pick from the defaults replaces it with one of your defaults.

Night

  • Fixed exposure — the ISO, aperture and shutter speed used all night. Set them for the sky and the place on the night (moon, light pollution).
  • Fixed-exposure sun altitude — how far below the horizon the sun must be for Night to begin and end.
  • Pre-night compensation (EV) — the brightness to aim for while going into the night.
  • Post-night compensation (EV) — the same for coming out of it. Useful when sunrise is shot dark but you want the dawn before it brighter.

Sunset and Sunrise

  • Exposure compensation (EV) — shooting darker keeps the colour of the sun but loses the landscape. How far to go is your call.
  • Sun altitude — where in the twilight this control ends (sunset) or begins (sunrise).
  • Exposure smoothing (this plan) — the light changes fast here, so these two can have their own smoothing. Global means “use the global setting”.

Daytime

Daytime can start from the bright side or the dark side, so you choose its Reference: Bright limit, Midpoint or Dark limit.

Exposure limits and range

Set the Bright limit and Dark limit of ISO, aperture and shutter speed. Drag the rows with the ⇅ handle to set the order: the top one moves first; when it reaches its limit the next one moves.

  • ISO on top: noise drops as soon as it gets light.
  • Aperture on top: stop down early for a sunstar.
  • Aperture at the bottom: vignetting stays the same for longer.

Exposure smoothing

Menu → Exposure smoothing sets the global values.

  • Hysteresis — the exposure does not move while the light stays within this band around the target. Wider ignores small flickers but reacts later.
  • Smoothness (min) — how long the exposure takes to reach its full rate of change. Longer eases each change and reduces flicker, but lags a little behind fast changes such as passing clouds.

6. Shooting with the phone camera

  1. Fix the phone to a tripod and frame the shot. Connect a power bank for a long shoot.
  2. Open the plan and choose the phone as the camera.
  3. Tap the start icon. The plan shows ● Shooting; if the start time is still ahead it shows ● Waiting to start.
  4. You can close the app. A “Shooting” notification shows the progress and has Stop.

The video goes into Movies/TwyLapse; DNG and JPEG frames go into Pictures/TwyLapse, in a folder for each shoot.

The phone camera can be used by one plan at a time. Some phones cannot move the focus; the app then sets what it can and tells you.

7. Shooting with a Canon camera

Needs the Shoot with an external camera add-on, or its trial (chapter 11).

Prepare the camera

  • Set the mode dial to M (manual) for stills. In movie mode, or on some models in any mode but M, the app waits and tells you.
  • Set Picture Style to something other than Auto — Auto can make the video flicker.
  • Insert a card with plenty of space, with the write-protect switch off.
  • Use a full battery or a mains adapter.
  • Set up the camera’s Wi-Fi for CCAPI. steps per camera / link to Canon

Connect

The phone and the camera must be on the same Wi-Fi network. Some ways to do that:

  • A travel router that both join (the most stable).
  • The phone’s hotspot. confirm
  • The access point of an external device (TLP-Edge-…, chapter 8).

Then use Look for cameras (in My cameras). A camera found for the first time can be added to My cameras straight away. If the camera asks for a login, enter it under Camera login (only if the camera needs one) in the camera’s settings.

Shoot

Pick the camera in the plan and tap start. Before the first frame the app settles the exposure (Settling the exposure before the shoot). Leave the camera alone until the shoot ends; at the end the camera’s settings are put back.

Several cameras

Each plan controls one camera; run several plans to use several cameras. Sync shooting with Extra cameras fires them together. Menu → Camera bookings shows which plan uses which camera when; overlapping plans are marked ✕ and cannot start. confirm limits

8. Using an external device

An external device (M5Stack CoreS3 or M5StickS3) stays by the camera and runs the shoot, so the phone can go home with you. Needs the Control the camera from an external device add-on.

Write the software (first time)

  1. Connect the device to the phone with a USB cable (the device end is USB-C; a charge-only cable will not work).
  2. Menu → Update firmware (USB). Check reads the device without writing; Write installs the software.
  3. If it cannot enter download mode, hold the power button for about two seconds, and press it once more when the LED blinks.

Register the device

  1. Menu → Device settings → + New device.
  2. The device shows a QR code. Tap Scan the device QR code and scan it.
  3. Give it a name (letters and digits only), choose the network, and press Send settings.

The phone becomes the device’s owner. A device registered to another phone has to be released there first.

Network

  • Make the device an access point — the device makes its own Wi-Fi (TLP-Edge-…). Join it from the phone and the camera. Handy outdoors.
  • Off — the device joins an existing network (enter its SSID and password).

Shoot

In the plan, set Controller to the device and start. The plan is sent to the device, which shoots on its own; the phone shows progress while it is in range and can stop it. While a plan is on the device it cannot be edited — use Remove from the device first.

9. After the shoot

Capture reports

Menu → Capture reports. One report per shoot: frames shot, frames on time, metering and exposure, and the measured interval against the one you set.

Findings suggest what to change next time, for example “Live view is not refreshing fast enough for this interval. A longer interval will be more stable.”

Activity history and logs

Activity history lists starts, stops and how each shoot ended. If you contact support about a problem, the Debug log helps us — Fetch logs saves it to the Download folder.

11. Add-ons, trial and refunds

Menu → Add-ons.

  • Shoot with an external camera — Try starts a trial of 10 shoots confirm. A shoot counts once at least one frame is taken; a shoot that never found the camera does not count. A shoot that is running finishes even if the trial runs out. Buy becomes available once the trial has started. Buying also removes the ads.
  • Control the camera from an external device — needs the add-on above. No trial.

When the trial is used up, the plans you made and the cameras you added stay; buy the add-on to use them again.

Refunds: within 48 hours of purchase you can request a refund yourself through Google Play. We cannot refund after that. On a new phone, sign in with the same Google account and your purchase comes back.

12. Other settings

  • Language — English, 日本語, or Match the phone.
  • Theme — Light, Dark or Match the phone.
  • Colors — the colours of each capture control in the schedule.
  • Locations, My cameras, My lenses — saved places and gear. A camera not in the list can be added with + Add a camera that is not listed.
  • Reset to factory settings — erases plans, gear, locations, logs and settings on this phone. Purchased add-ons stay. Settings on external devices are not touched. This cannot be undone.
